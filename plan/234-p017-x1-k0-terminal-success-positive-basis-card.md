# Plan 234: P017 X1 K0 Typed Terminal-Success Positive-Basis Card

## Role and authority

This LAB ordinary-design comparison follows Plan 233. It compares exactly one
fact role, typed terminal owner success, for one K0 V1/R1 cross-locus read.
It keeps A-Sigma and B-Pi as separate retained presentations. Canon remains
normative. This card does not accept a new semantic fact, amend P017, or
change the all-`OPEN` status recorded by Plan 233.

The purpose is narrower than an owner-branch model: determine the smallest
candidate-native positive basis that a later ordinary candidate could use to
close this one fact-status cell. The result is an advisory A/A recommendation,
not an owner disposition. A later candidate must still name and accept its own
`H_K` hypothesis before Plan 233 can be rerun with a non-`OPEN` status.

This card selects no relation arity, carrier, field, record, tuple, relation
family or role index, identity/equality rule, request/result/receipt object,
failure member/row, validation or mutation algorithm, transition, lifecycle,
occurrence, causal generator, `Config`/`SaveObject` placement, restore
function, source/elaboration form, runtime, theorem/OBL, conformance, Gate,
Phase, sample, implementation, wire/API, or public behavior.

## Direct constraints and status quo

P017 X1 requires an explicit relation-valued exchange component, an owner
outstanding/terminal service distinction, typed owner result or typed
owner-service failure facts, and a separation of owner service from requester
receipt/use. It does not select the fact's semantic representation. Plan 227
requires candidate-native `C + H_K + D_K` material. Plan 232 retains the
non-erasable A-Sigma/B-Pi primitive presentation delta. Plan 233 permits a
typed-terminal-success cell to close only through either a positive native
terminal-success membership or a non-circular erasable derivation from positive
typed-result membership and a local terminality premise.

The status quo is therefore unchanged:

| Presentation | Typed terminal owner success | Other B fact roles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A-Sigma | `OPEN` | outstanding, typed failure, and consulted provenance are `OPEN` |
| B-Pi | `OPEN` | outstanding, typed failure, and consulted provenance are `OPEN` |

`q` is one existing request occurrence in its current or admissibly restored
history. It is not source spelling, a public/transport token, or global
cross-load identity. `v : tau` below is only metanotation for a value with an
already available theory/02 type; it does not introduce a result object,
carrier, or new type constructor. All statements below are non-exhaustive fact
views, not fields, constructors, or a lifecycle enumeration.

## Candidate A: direct positive terminal-success membership

### A-Sigma

Candidate A states, as metanotation only, that the primitive whole `q`-scoped
restriction positively contains one typed owner-terminal-success fact:

```text
Sigma_q positively contains owner-terminal-success(q, v : tau)
```

Typing and terminality are intrinsic to that one positive membership. The
statement is legal only if it is a non-exhaustive native view of the whole
slice, rather than a tag, field, tuple/record position, constructor, fixed
relation role, or complete family of views.

### B-Pi

Candidate A states independently that the plural `q`-anchored presentation
positively contains a native typed owner-terminal-success membership:

```text
Pi positively contains, at q, owner-terminal-success(q, v : tau)
```

The statement is legal only if it names neither a carrying-family identifier,
factor role, enumerated family, shared carrier/key, common result/branch record,
membership witness, nor result identity. Terminality must be intrinsic to the
one native positive membership, not inferred from another fact.

### Conditional classification

If a future ordinary candidate explicitly adopts the respective statement as
its `H_K`, Plan 233 classifies that candidate's typed-terminal-success cell as
`primitive`. This is a conditional ledger result, not a claim that either
membership already exists in Canon or in a completed X1 model.

The typed value content is sufficient for this *fact-status comparison*; it
does not decide whether a later complete P017 package needs a separately useful
owner-result view, nor does it choose a result carrier. A direct success fact
must never be weakened into an untyped terminal assertion and then treated as
P017's typed-result requirement.

## Candidate B: typed result plus local terminality derivation

### A-Sigma

Candidate B instead posits a positive typed owner-result fact in the whole
slice and one constitutive, `q`-local static clause:

```text
Sigma_q positively contains owner-result(q, v : tau)
every such membership denotes terminal completion of owner service for that q
```

Under those exact premises, `owner-terminal-success(q, v : tau)` would be an
erasable derived view. The terminality clause must not be a second primitive
terminal fact, a transition, a reachability/lifecycle assertion, or a circular
restatement of the target.

### B-Pi

Candidate B separately posits a native `q`-anchored owner-result membership and
a constitutive clause applying directly to every such membership:

```text
Pi positively contains, at q, owner-result(q, v : tau)
each such membership denotes terminal owner-service success for that q and v
```

Applying the clause must require no pairing of distinct memberships,
carrying-family identification, shared result/branch witness, factor role,
result identity, or conversion of coherence into semantic fact residence.

### Conditional classification

If the corresponding positive result membership and non-circular local clause
are both explicitly adopted as `H_K`, and the uniqueness test below passes,
Plan 233 classifies that candidate's cell as `uniquely derived`. No such
candidate is accepted by this card.

## Candidate C: retain OPEN

Candidate C adds neither positive basis. The corresponding Plan 233 cell
remains `OPEN`. C is mandatory whenever A or B crosses one of the stated stop
conditions. It is a conservative disposition, not an assertion that terminal
success is absent.

## Consumer, uniqueness test, and falsifiers

The only immediate consumers are the two typed-terminal-success cells in Plan
233: A-Sigma and B-Pi. The only later bookkeeping consumer is its existing
owner-success frontier inventory: a future L package may know whether it must
preserve one primitive fact or the declared derivation premises. This card does
not supply persistence or restoration behavior.

For Candidate B, hold fixed all positive typed owner-result memberships and
the permitted local static constraints. If two admissible interpretations can
still disagree about terminal owner-service success for a result, the
derivation is not unique and Candidate B fails.

| Candidate / presentation | Decisive falsifier |
| --- | --- |
| A / A-Sigma | Stating the fact needs a tag, field, tuple/record position, constructor, fixed role, exhaustive view family, absence of failure, or an unselected dynamic condition. |
| A / B-Pi | Stating the fact needs a carrying-family/index, shared carrier/key, common record/witness, result identity, factor role, or inferred rather than intrinsic terminality. |
| B / A-Sigma | The local clause is circular, adds another subject/choice/witness/identity/package, uses absence, or invokes transition, lifecycle, occurrence, or reachability. |
| B / B-Pi | Applying the clause pairs memberships, finds a family, uses a shared result/branch witness, converts coherence into residence, or invokes dynamic incompatibility/transition/reachability. |
| C | A valid candidate-native A or B basis displaces C for that one candidate cell; missing evidence does not make C false. |

Neither A nor B may be used to infer terminal success/failure exclusivity,
absence of owner failure, no-owner-mutation, validation/authority success,
requester receipt/acceptance/use, occurrence/causal order, persistence,
restore, one-shot behavior, P017 satisfaction, theorem/OBL closure, or runtime
behavior. In particular, the Plan 233 case where success and failure both
appear remains an open dynamic incompatibility obligation.

## Comparative result and advisory recommendation

The three alternatives have the following local cost:

| Alternative | Positive assumptions for this one cell | Result if later adopted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | one native typed terminal-success membership | `primitive` |
| B | typed result membership plus a local terminality clause | `uniquely derived` |
| C | no positive basis | `OPEN` |

For this one consumer, the smallest admissible conditional candidate is A in
both presentations. It directly expresses the fact Plan 233 asks about and
does not add B's terminality clause. The advisory recommendation is therefore
**A/A**, subject to each presentation's independent falsifier.

This is not a conclusion that the final language should expose an owner success
primitive, nor that a complete X1 design should lack a reusable result view.
Those would require further ordinary design work, including the remaining B,
T, U, C, and L obligations. Until an ordinary candidate adopts a basis, the
repository-wide Plan 233 status remains eight `OPEN` rows.

## Work classification, stop, and next boundary

This is ordinary LAB design-card work, not an L3 experiment: both legal basis
forms already appear in Plan 233; the only immediate consumer is the same
fact-status ledger; and a fixture or Lean artifact would merely encode a design
declaration while prohibited transition/persistence vocabulary remains absent.

Stop and retain `OPEN` for the affected presentation if a falsifier is reached.
Also stop if the card begins to choose a schema, result carrier, failure row,
validation/mutation algorithm, transition, occurrence/causal rule, persistence
realization, proof, runtime, or public contract. Do not repair a failed B-Pi
form by adding a hidden family index or package.

The next autonomous question is a source-conformance audit: whether a direct
typed terminal-success membership can serve P017's stated typed owner-result
fact without silently selecting a result carrier or a separately reusable
result view. That audit may only clarify the requirement boundary; it may not
turn this advisory A/A recommendation into a Canon or model acceptance.
